Atoms — a nano sandbox

Interactive molecular dynamics: gas, liquid, crystal, phase transitions and chemical reactions emerge from interaction potentials and conservation laws, not from prepared scripts. This manual describes version 4.0.

This manual uses the labels of the English interface. The program is also fully available in Russian: the RU | EN buttons in the top bar or Ctrl+L switch the language (see Interface language); the Russian manual is ИНСТРУКЦИЯ.html. Every button, slider and atom also shows a tooltip on hover.

Quick start

  • Tab — scene menu. Pick any scene: it starts right away.
  • Space — pause, R — reset the scene, Ctrl+LMB — rotate the camera.
  • E — periodic table: pick an element, then LMB in the scene to add atoms.
  • LMB on an atom — "tweezers". RMB — heat, Shift+RMB — cool, wheel — zoom.
  • H — a short key cheat sheet inside the program, F1 — the full manual in the interface language (this file when the interface is English).
  • Ctrl+L or the RU | EN buttons — switch the interface language instantly.
  • Hover over any button, slider or atom to see a tooltip.

Screen

Top: the command bar. Scenes, Table (periodic table), pause/step/reset/undo, open/save, snapshot, frame recording, side panel, Help, and the RU | EN language switch. On the right: model time (ps), step number and frames per second.
Left: tools (what LMB does in the scene). Icons with tooltips; quick selection with Alt+digit.
Top right: readings: T, P, density ρ (σ⁻³ and g/cm³), N (number of atoms), total energy E, external work W, energy drift, Ek/Ep/Ebond, time step dt, phase with a phase fraction bar, and stability alerts. Everything in both model and real units. Values that need attention are shown on a light plate; errors are inverted (see Colors).
Right: tabs: Control (Thermostat, Pressure and boundaries, Thermal walls, System, Chemistry, Integration and tools, Display), Physics, Chemistry, Plots, Object (inspector for the selection, field objects, measurements and the atom under the cursor).

The scene background is black; you see a thin box frame, axes and depth shading, and a scale bar in nm in the corner. G hides the side panel, F11 or Alt+Enter — full screen. The interface scales from 1280×720 up to 4K.

Colors

The interface is monochrome: panels, buttons, text, plots, the grid, toasts and field objects use shades of gray only, so that color always means something about the atoms.

  • Atoms keep their colors: element (CPK) colors and every coloring mode (C) — element, speed, energy, order, coordination, phase. The legend in the scene corner, the palette swatches, the phase fraction bar in the readings and the color square on the element card are colored too, so every atom color can be read off.
  • Plot series are told apart by line style — solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot — and brightness; each legend shows a sample of the line.
  • Warnings appear on a light plate (e.g. energy drift above 0.2 %, a reduced time step, capped forces); a stability alert is a white-text line marked with a white bar. Errors are inverted: a white plate with black text (e.g. energy drift above 1 %).
  • An active or selected control has a white frame. Heat and energy release are drawn bright and solid; cold and absorption dim and dashed. Partial charges (charges on the Control tab): + — a bright solid ring, − — a gray dashed ring.

Interface language

  • The interface is available in English and Russian. The RU | EN buttons in the top bar or Ctrl+L switch the language instantly, without a restart: labels, tooltips, toasts, scene titles and the cheat sheet all change.
  • The choice is saved in atoms.ini next to atoms.exe. On the first start the language follows the Windows UI language.
  • Command line: atoms.exe --lang en or --lang ru sets the language for that run (it takes priority over atoms.ini and is not saved).
  • F1 opens MANUAL.html when the interface is English and ИНСТРУКЦИЯ.html when it is Russian (if one file is missing, the other one opens).
  • CSV export follows the language: English uses a comma separator and a decimal point, Russian a semicolon and a decimal comma (for Excel with a Russian locale). Column headers are in the interface language.

Everything you do by hand (heating, pushing, shock waves, breaking bonds, inserting and deleting, field objects, resizing the box) is counted as external work W. So E − W is conserved, and its "drift" shows how honest the integration is.

Field objects

Permanent "instruments" inside the box. Tool Alt+F; pick the object type on the small panel at the left edge of the scene. LMB — place it; wind and source — drag to set the direction; barrier — drag from point to point. An object is placed on the plane through the box center, perpendicular to the view. Object zones and icons are drawn in gray.

ObjectEffect
attractorA smooth potential well of radius R — gathers atoms.
repellerA potential "hill" — pushes atoms apart.
heaterA local Langevin thermostat: atoms inside R approach the zone temperature T.
coolerThe same with a low T — a condenser, a "cold finger".
windA constant force along a direction within R — a flow.
vortexA tangential force — swirls the matter.
trapA harmonic well — holds particles (optical tweezers).
sourceEmits atoms of the chosen element at a set rate and speed (the element comes from the periodic table or the palette).
sinkAbsorbs molecules that get inside — pumping out.
barrierAn impenetrable wall plate; its normal and size are set on the Object tab.

Over an object: wheel — radius, Shift+wheel — strength, N — on/off, Del or RMB — delete, drag — move. All parameters (radius R, strength A, zone T, element, coordinates in nm) and the buttons on/off, copy, delete are on the Object tab. A new object switches on smoothly (≈1τ) to avoid a jolt.

Selection and measurements

  • Box — select, Shift — add to the selection, Ctrl+A — everything. Clicking an atom selects its whole molecule.
  • Drag the selection — move it; Alt+drag — "throw" (set a velocity); Alt+wheel — rotate.
  • Del — delete, Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V / Ctrl+X — copy / paste at the cursor / cut.
  • I — pin/unpin: pinned atoms stay still (a substrate, a clamp, an "anchor") and do not count toward the temperature.
  • The Object tab: composition, T (excl. CM), center of mass, momentum |p|, mass; buttons pin/unpin, stop, heat ×1.5 T, cool ×0.67 T, element → X (X is the current palette substance), delete, copy, paste, rotate −15° / rotate +15°, follow, deselect.
  • Moving, rotating, pasting and changing the element are refused if atoms would overlap, so the sandbox does not "explode". element → X checks this after the replacement: if the new atoms would overlap their neighbors, the change cancels itself and a toast says so.
  • Ruler (Alt+3): the Measurement block on the Object tab shows distance 1–2, angle 1–2–3 and the dihedral angle.

Camera

  • Ctrl+LMB — rotate, Ctrl+Shift+LMB — pan, wheel — zoom, arrows — rotate, PgUp/PgDn — closer/farther.
  • F/Home — fit the box, O — auto-rotation, F2 front, F3 side, F4 top, F6 isometric.
  • V — fly mode: WASD, Q/E — down/up, Shift — faster.
  • X — cross-section, Shift+wheel — position of the cutting plane.

Physics

Model

  • Van der Waals — the Lennard-Jones potential (Lorentz–Berthelot rules), shifted cutoff at 2.5σ.
  • Electrostatics — screened (Yukawa) Coulomb with force shifting; atomic charges come from electronegativities.
  • Covalent bonds — Morse potential with a soft core, multiple bonds and VSEPR bond angles: tetrahedral for sp³ carbon, silicon, NH₄⁺ and BH₄⁻, pyramidal for NH₃, PH₃ and H₃O⁺, flat for BF₃ and sp² carbon, linear for CO₂ and acetylene.
  • Hydrogen bonds — the directional DREIDING term; water also has a three-body tetrahedral term (as in mW): ice and water anomalies.
  • Metals — the many-body second-moment potential (Gupta / Cleri–Rosato): a bond depends on the number of neighbors, so surfaces, melting and nanoparticle faceting are realistic.
  • Integrator — velocity Verlet with an automatic time step, Verlet neighbor list, OpenMP. Forces are checked against numerical differentiation of the energy (relative error ~10⁻⁷).

Units

Model units are argon: σ = 0.3405 nm, mass 10 u. The energy ε/k = 139.8 K is calibrated so that the model's critical point (LJ with a 2.5σ cutoff, T*c = 1.078) matches argon at 150.7 K. The triple point then comes out at ≈ 87 K (argon: 83.8 K), time τ = 0.999 ps, pressure ε/σ³ = 482.5 atm. Chemical scale: 1 eV = 4ε. The program shows both model and real quantities everywhere.

Thermostats and barostat

  • NVE — an isolated system, energy is conserved.
  • Bussi (CSVR) and Langevin — a correct canonical ensemble; Nosé–Hoover — deterministic; Berendsen — fast, gentle approach to T; power heating — constant power (for the melting plateau, set with the heating power slider).
  • NPT barostat (C-rescale) on the Physics tab (Bernetti–Bussi): the box "breathes" toward the set pressure P* external with correct volume fluctuations. Periodic boundaries only; with walls there is a piston.
  • Pressure comes from the virial theorem with all contributions (pair, many-body, bonds, angles).

External influences

gravity g, electric field E along x (field E and current I in the readings), Thermal walls (hot left / cold right, or a hot bottom), wall wetting, field objects, tools.

Stability guard

The program keeps two state snapshots (every 200 steps). If non-finite numbers appear or the temperature "explodes" with a broken energy balance, it rolls back to a snapshot, reduces the step and continues. An alert line in the readings, marked with a white bar, reports this; the Integration stability block on the Physics tab counts the rollbacks.

States of matter

  • Each atom gets a phase — gas, liquid, solid — from its local structure (Steinhardt order parameters), number of neighbors and mobility. The phase coloring mode (C) colors atoms by it.
  • The readings show a phase fraction bar (in the phase colors) and a description of the state ("melting: crystal + liquid", "evaporation: liquid + vapor" and so on).
  • The Physics tab: a mini T–ρ phase diagram of the model with the trace of your state. Solid gray line — the liquid–vapor binodal, white dashed — melting/freezing, fine dots — the triple line, C — the critical point; a white square with a dot is the current state and a light line its path. Below it: State of matter, the Transition log (melting, crystallization, boiling, condensation, sublimation) with temperature and time, and the heat of transition from the T plateau.
  • The order coloring mode shows the lattice: FCC, HCP, BCC, SC/NaCl, ice.
Try this: scene 2 → thermostat power heating → watch the T(t) plateau on the Plots tab and the transition log. Then place a cooler (Alt+F) and watch crystallization start from the cold zone.

Chemistry

How reactions happen

Chemistry here is not an animation but events within the same molecular dynamics: a bond forms when atoms come close and have a free valence; it breaks when overstretched; an atom switches to another partner (exchange) if the barrier is overcome. The energy of each event is computed exactly: reaction heat goes into atomic motion, so combustion heats the mixture by itself, and endothermic reactions cool it.

  • Bond energies and lengths — real tabulated values for the common pairs and bond orders (H–H 4.52 eV, O=O 5.12, N≡N 9.79, C–H 4.3, O–H 4.8…). Halogens, sulfur, phosphorus, silicon, boron and interhalogen pairs (H–F, N–F, P–F, Si–F, B–F, S–Cl, B–Cl, Cl–F, I–Cl, H–Se, H–Ge, H–As and others, about 70 pairs in all) also come from reference tables; the remaining pairs follow Pauling's rules from electronegativities. Scale: 1 eV = 4ε.
  • Barriers — the Evans–Polanyi rule: Ea = E₀ + α·ΔH, but not below ΔH. The reaction barriers × slider changes the kinetics without touching the thermodynamics.
  • Charges — partial, from electronegativity differences; for ions (H₃O⁺, OH⁻, NH₄⁺…) the charge is distributed by atomic hardness (a simplified electronegativity equalization).
  • Acids and bases — proton transfer: HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻, H⁺ hopping along a chain of water molecules (Grotthuss), neutralization H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O, NH₃ + H₃O⁺ → NH₄⁺. Water autoionization is not modeled: in a box of ~10⁻²³ L its equilibrium is zero ions.
  • Catalysis — a catalyst zone (K; barriers ×0.25) and the surface of metals Pt, Pd, Ni, Fe, Cu, Ru, Rh, Ag… (×0.3): only the kinetic part of the barrier is lowered.
  • Metals with non-metals — oxides and salts through ionic-covalent bonds; an oxidized atom loses its metallic bonding. An atom's oxidation state is shown on the Chemistry tab (Atom: charges and oxidation) on hover.
  • Flash of light (L) — photodissociation of weak bonds (Cl₂ → 2Cl starts a chain reaction).

The Chemistry tab

  • Reactions: switches chemical reactions, acids and bases, metal surface catalysis, catalyst zone, the reaction barriers × slider, event and heat counters.
  • Acidity (pH) — a pH scale with H₃O⁺, OH⁻ and water concentrations in mol/L.
  • Molecule and structure library — 127 structures in four sections. Collapsed, it shows one row per section; full library (127) expands it, collapse folds it back.
    • inorganic molecules (46): H₂O, H₂O₂, CO₂, CO, NH₃, HCl, HF, HBr, HI, H₂S, PH₃, SiH₄, HCN, N₂H₄, O₃, NO, NO₂, HNO₂, NH₂OH, NH₂Cl, HOCl, Cl₂O, OF₂, NF₃, NCl₃, PF₃, PCl₃, AsH₃, H₂Se, SCl₂, CS₂, COS, SiF₄, SiCl₄, GeH₄, BF₃, BCl₃, N₂, O₂, H₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂, ClF, ICl;
    • organic molecules (52): alkanes from methane to hexane and isobutane; ethylene, propylene, butadiene, acetylene, propyne; cyclopentane and cyclohexane; benzene, phenol, toluene, styrene, aniline, benzoic acid, naphthalene, pyridine; methanol, ethanol, isopropanol, ethylene glycol, glycerol; dimethyl and diethyl ether; formaldehyde, acetaldehyde, acetone; formic, acetic, oxalic and lactic acids, ethyl acetate; acetonitrile, formamide; chloromethanes, chloroethane, vinyl chloride, CF₄, Freon-12; methanethiol, methylamine, urea, glycine, alanine;
    • ions (12): H₃O⁺, OH⁻, NH₄⁺, CN⁻, HS⁻, ClO⁻, NO₂⁻, HCOO⁻, CH₃COO⁻, HCO₃⁻, NaOH, NaCl (ion pair);
    • crystals and metal clusters (17): NaCl crystal, diamond, graphene, fullerene C₆₀, ice, silicon crystal, SiO₂, and Pt, Au, Fe, Ni, Cu, Ag, Al, Pd, Rh, Pb clusters.
    Rings (benzene, pyridine, naphthalene, cyclohexane) are built first and their substituents grow from them along VSEPR directions; fullerene is a truncated icosahedron with single bonds on the pentagons and double bonds between hexagons. After insertion every structure is relaxed right away. Pick a structure — it goes into the first palette cell — then LMB in the scene, or use to box center / 5 at random spots.
  • Mixture composition — molecular formulas, their counts and charges.
  • Reaction log — equations, event counts, ΔH in kJ/mol and eV. Click a reaction to select it.
  • Kinetics and equilibrium for the selected reaction: reaction rate (events per τ and per ps), rate constant k (L/(mol·s)), the reverse reaction next to it, equilibrium constant Kc from the current concentrations, and Ea (Arrhenius).
Experiments: "Acid in water" → watch the pH and the H+ transfer counter on the Chemistry tab. "Platinum catalysis" → erase the platinum with the eraser and the mixture stops reacting. "Chemical equilibrium" → move the piston and watch the Cl / Cl₂ fractions shift (Le Chatelier's principle). "Equilibrium H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI" → select the reaction in the log and watch Kc settle.
The model is simplified: one bond curve per atom pair, no quantum effects and no explicit electron transfer (redox goes through bonds). Reaction rates are sped up, otherwise nothing would happen within the available simulation time.

Periodic table

E (or Table in the top bar) — all 118 elements. Categories are told apart by gray level and the pattern of the strip at the top of each cell (solid, dashed, dotted); the legend below the table explains them, and radioactive elements carry a small triangle in the corner. Clicking selects the element: it becomes the first palette cell, and if a source field object is selected, it becomes the source's element.

Hovering shows the element card:

  • symbol, name, category, atomic mass, "radioactive"; a small colored square shows the atom's color in the scene;
  • electronegativity χ, valence, covalent radius r_cov (for metals also r_met);
  • group and period, or "period N · f-element" for lanthanides and actinides;
  • the ground-state electron configuration with a noble-gas core, e.g. [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² for iron, including the known exceptions such as Cr [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹, Cu [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹, Pd [Kr] 4d¹⁰ and Au [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s¹;
  • melting point and boiling point in °C; for C and As, which do not melt at 1 atm, the sublimation point;
  • density ρ in g/cm³ (for gases in g/L), ionization energy (the first one, eV) and the state at 20 °C (solid, liquid or gas);
  • how the element works in the model.

Values for At, Fr and Z ≥ 99 are estimates and are shown with "≈". For Z ≥ 104 the card says the properties of the element are not measured: its atoms live seconds or less.

  • Noble gases — van der Waals only.
  • Metals — many-body metallic bonding; with non-metals they form ionic-covalent bonds (oxides, halides).
  • Non-metals — covalent bonds by Pauling's rules (the energy grows with the electronegativity difference), multiple bonds for B, C, N, O, Si, P, S, Ge, As, Se.
  • Radioactive elements are marked, but decay is not modeled.

Files, snapshots, export

  • Saving (Ctrl+S, F5) — the whole state: atoms, bonds, pins, field objects, parameters, view. Older files still open.
  • Snapshot — F12: a PNG of the scene next to atoms.exe; Shift+F12 — the whole window; Ctrl+F12 — record frames into a folder named frames_… (кадры_… with the Russian interface); you can assemble a video from it.
  • CSV — Ctrl+E: plots of T, P, energies, g(r), speed distribution, MSD and molecule counts, for Excel. With the English interface the separator is a comma and the decimal mark a point; with the Russian one — a semicolon and a comma.
  • atoms.ini next to atoms.exe stores the interface language.

What's new in 4.0

  • The model is three-dimensional only. The flat mode and the D key are gone; the code for it was removed from physics, chemistry, analysis and rendering. Old files saved in the flat mode are refused with a message.
  • 8 new scenes (46 in all): Liquid and vapor, Adsorption, Temperature equalization, Cavitation, Nanowire tension, Propane combustion, NCl₃ explosion, Autoignition (with an H₂ + Cl₂ variant). Most of them have a live measurement line.
  • Molecule library: 127 structures instead of 65 — nitrogen and halogen compounds, boron and silicon halides, rings (cyclohexane, naphthalene, pyridine, styrene, aniline, benzoic acid), alcohols, acids, esters, amino acids, seven new ions, fullerene C₆₀ and Rh and Pb clusters. Rings are built as rings and their substituents grow along VSEPR directions.

Version 3.0

  • Bilingual interface. English and Russian; RU | EN in the top bar or Ctrl+L switch instantly. The choice is saved in atoms.ini; on the first start the language follows Windows; --lang ru|en on the command line. F1 opens the manual in the interface language; CSV export uses the separators of that language.
  • Monochrome interface. Panels, buttons, text, plots, the grid, toasts and field objects are gray; only atoms and the legends explaining their colors are colored. Plot series differ by line style; warnings sit on a light plate, errors are inverted (white plate, black text). The phase diagram uses a solid gray binodal, white dashed melting lines, a dotted triple line and a white square for the current state.
  • Scene menu in two sections — "Matter: phases, transport, mechanics" and "Chemistry and solutions" — and 18 new scenes: Heat conduction, Shock tube (with Joule expansion), Barometric formula, Effusion, Nanoparticle sintering, Adiabatic compression, Wetting (with non-wetting), Seeded crystallization, Poiseuille flow, Methane chlorination, Equilibrium H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, Hydrogenation on nickel, Peroxide decomposition, Acetylene combustion, H₂ + Br₂ in light, Chlorine displaces bromine, Hydrogen and fluorine, Ozone decomposition. The new physics scenes show a live measurement line; field objects placed by a scene are kept correctly when it loads; "NaCl in water" and "Iron oxidation" were reworked.
  • Molecule library: 65 structures instead of 32, in four sections, with a collapsed one-row-per-section view; bond data for H–F, H–Br, H–I, H–S, H–P, H–Si, F–F, Br–Br, I–I, C–F, C–Br, C–I, Si–Si from reference tables; a new reaction rate row in the reaction kinetics.
  • Element card: group and period, electron configuration, melting and boiling (or sublimation) point, density, ionization energy and the state at 20 °C.
